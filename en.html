Duong Phuc Khanh (Vietnamese: Dương Phúc Khánh, born 1995 in Da Nang, Vietnam; canonical handle: khanhdp, moniker: Kido) is a Vietnamese Senior Software Engineer and Systems Architect. An alumnus of the University of Economics - The University of Da Nang and First Prize winner of Startup Runway 2018 with the JustOrder smart food ordering platform [1], he served for six years as Tech Lead and Project Manager at FPT Software SBI Lab (consecutively awarded the Best Performance Team Award in 2022, 2023, and 2024), and is the creator of the Kido Chronicles production engineering war journal (128+ episodes) [3], the Winter Arc mobile web platform (1.1K+ WAU) [2], and the Agent Harness Kit autonomous multi-agent engineering framework.

Known for his pragmatic systems design methodology titled the "7-Rung Ladder" (Ponytail Mindset), Khanh specializes in high-throughput distributed locking, database query acceleration, zero-TBT mobile Web performance, and multi-agent coordination. His engineering research works and software suites are registered internationally under ORCID identifier 0009-0005-7192-2664 and indexed within Wikidata (Q141718572) and the Google Knowledge Graph (/g/11zyg5zq1c).

01. Early Life & Education

Duong Phuc Khanh was born in 1995 in Da Nang, the economic, scientific, and technological hub of Central Vietnam. From 2013 to 2017, he pursued his undergraduate education at the Faculty of Statistics and Information Systems, University of Economics - The University of Da Nang (DUE), graduating with a Bachelor of Science in Management Information Systems (MIS).

During his undergraduate tenure, Khanh focused his research on the intersection of economics, database management systems, and early cloud computing models. He developed a strong foundation in relational data structures, algorithmic complexity, and asynchronous network protocols. This interdisciplinary training—uniting business domain comprehension with deep systems programming—became the defining trademark of his later engineering career.

02. Engineering Career

2.1 Startup Roots: JustOrder & Startup Runway Champion (2018–2019)

In early 2018, recognizing the persistent bottlenecks faced by hospitality venues in Da Nang during peak dining hours, Khanh co-founded JustOrder, an early QR-code and mobile food ordering system [1]. Serving as the technical lead, he architected a real-time ordering and kitchen ticket routing engine designed to operate reliably under heavy retail concurrency.

"Patrons simply open the app or scan the QR code placed on the dining table to view the digital menu, customize items, and transmit orders directly into kitchen displays. The platform eliminates table wait times, elevates dining experiences, and empowers restaurant owners with real-time cash flow and inventory tracking." — As reported in Báo Đà Nẵng (Da Nang Newspaper), July 10, 2018 [1]

On June 9, 2018, at the Grand Finale of Startup Runway 2018—an entrepreneurship competition co-organized by the University of Economics (The University of Da Nang) and the Cork Institute of Technology (CIT, Ireland)—the JustOrder project led by Duong Phuc Khanh won First Prize (Champion). In addition to a cash prize, the team was awarded an intensive startup incubation scholarship and training tour in Ireland [1].

04. Architectural Philosophy: The 7-Rung Ladder & "Ponytail" Pragmatism

In software engineering practice, Duong Phuc Khanh advocates a radical form of pragmatic minimalism known as the "7-Rung Ladder" (Ponytail Mindset), anchored by the axiom: "The best code is the code you never wrote; lazy means efficient, not careless."

The 7-Rung Verification Ladder Before Writing Code:
1. YAGNI: Does this need to exist at all? Speculative requirement = skip it immediately.
2. Already in Codebase: Can we reuse existing helpers, repositories, or shared domain DTOs?
3. Standard Library: Does the JS/TS/Node/Bun standard library provide this natively?
4. Native Platform Feature: Can HTML5/CSS3 native controls or SQL constraints/indexes replace app-level code?
5. Installed Dependency: Leverage existing libraries in package.json; never install dependencies blindly.
6. One-Liner: Can the solution be expressed in a single, elegant line of code?
7. Absolute Minimum: Write the minimal necessary code, accompanied by rigorous automated regression tests.
